# Libertas Alpha Water Project (LAWP)

> The Master Venture Blueprint for the Libertas Alpha Network

## 1. Executive Summary

The Libertas Alpha Water Project (LAWP) is the foundational pilot Micro Venture Initiative (MVI) and the Master Venture Blueprint for the entire Libertas Alpha Network. It is engineered to fundamentally transform local water consumption culture, beginning with the FUTO environs, by establishing a profitable, scalable, and decentralized economic system that is legally and structurally bound to a non-profit mandate.

The core innovation of the LAWP is its direct and profitable solution to the environmental crisis caused by single-use plastic water sachets. The project promotes a circular economy model centered on a durable 18.9L dispenser can and a continuous refill culture.

### Multi-Signature Community Treasury

We are building a Multi-Signature Community Treasury Smart Contract for all financial flows. This smart contract is hard-coded to ensure that funds are never distributed as prohibited private profit. Financial returns to contributors are legally classified as:

- **Contributed Resource Recovery (RoC):** The return of the principal capital locked in inventory.
- **Impact Operational Grants:** Verifiable compensation for Human Nodes providing essential resources and technical execution, classified as a legal operational expense.

> **Note:** This is not an investment schema. Rather, people commit to this vision and are rewarded with an **Impact Token**. This token is the receipt of the money contributed to a pool. When the pool generates revenue, the immutable smart contract logic routes the contributed capital back to the contributor (minus a risk fee, usually 10%), providing continuous grants as the pool's revenue grows.

This framework ensures transparency, risk mitigation, and full legal compliance while driving measurable social achievement.

## 3. Financial Flow & Classifications

### 3.1 Pool Contribution

**Purpose:**
A Pool Contribution is the initial capital commitment made by a Human Node to the protocol. These contributed funds are pooled together and deployed to finance the acquisition of real-world assets, such as water dispensers and related operational infrastructure, enabling the delivery of clean water services.

Unlike a donation or speculative investment, a Pool Contribution functions as productive operational liquidity. The contributed capital is actively utilized by the protocol to acquire, manage, and maintain revenue-generating assets that support long-term ecosystem sustainability.

**Financial Flow:**
Once contributed, the capital enters the protocol's liquidity pool and is allocated toward approved operational activities, including:

1. Acquisition of water dispenser inventory and supporting infrastructure.
2. Deployment and expansion of clean water access points.
3. Funding of protocol operations necessary to deliver measurable social impact.

As the assets generate operational revenue through the refill system, the protocol follows a predefined financial flow:

1. Operational expenses and verified technical compensation are paid.
2. The contributor's principal is returned through the Return of Contributed Capital (RoC) mechanism, net of the applicable Risk Fee.
3. Verified social impact may result in Impact Operational Grants or Impact Tokens, depending on protocol rules.

**Classification:**
A Pool Contribution is not an equity investment, loan, or deposit. It is a contribution of operational liquidity that enables the protocol to deploy real-world assets, generate measurable impact, and sustainably recover contributed capital.

### 3.2 Return of Contributed Capital (RoC)

**Purpose:**
The Return of Contributed Capital (RoC) represents the recovery of the principal capital (liquidity) that was committed to funding inventory and operational assets within the ecosystem.

Before capital is returned, the protocol deducts the **Risk Fee**, which covers protocol-level operational risks, asset management, and ecosystem sustainability. As a result, contributors receive their original capital less the applicable Risk Fee.

**Classification:**
RoC is not a profit distribution, investment yield, or financial return. It is simply the return of the contributor's original capital, net of the Risk Fee, after the capital has fulfilled its operational purpose within the protocol.

### 3.3 Impact Operational Grants

**Purpose:**
Impact Operational Grants provide verifiable compensation to Human Nodes for supplying essential resources, including capital (liquidity) and technical execution required to operate and maintain the LAWP ecosystem.

**Classification:**
These grants are legally classified as necessary operational expenses, compensating contributors for measurable work and resources rather than representing investment returns or profit distributions.

## 4. Financial Architecture: The Value Flow Schematic

The LAWP financial architecture is designed to ensure both capital preservation and long-term operational sustainability. Contributors provide operational liquidity through Pool Contributions, which are deployed into real-world water infrastructure. Revenue generated from the ecosystem follows two sequential financial systems:

1. **The Bottle Activator System:** Recovers contributed capital and funds the initial operational cycle.
2. **The Refill System:** Provides recurring operational funding, ecosystem growth, and continuous compensation for verified contributors.

### 4.1 System 1: The Bottle Activator System (Initial Capital Cycle)

The Bottle Activator System governs the financial flow generated from the initial deployment and sale of each 18.9L dispenser can (Activator). Its primary objectives are to recover the contributor's deployed capital, fund the protocol's operational activities, and establish the foundation for long-term sustainability.

**Distribution of Operational Surplus:**

- **Human Node (30%):** Initial Impact Operational Grant. Provides verifiable operational compensation to the Human Node for supplying the initial operational liquidity and supporting protocol execution during the deployment phase.
- **Project Management / LA2 (50%):** Funds logistics, inventory management, operational overhead, field deployment, and serves as the funding source for the dynamic MVI DApp Team Grant.
- **System Treasury / MVI1 (20%):** Supports governance, protocol development, ecosystem expansion, compliance initiatives, and future Master Venture Implementations (MVIs).

**Dynamic MVI DApp Team Grant:**
Funded from the Project Management allocation, it is distributed according to real-time technical requirements:

- Platform maintenance
- Security updates
- Infrastructure scaling
- Product development
- Operational support

**Capital Recovery:**
Following the successful sale of the Activator, the protocol initiates the Return of Contributed Capital (RoC). The Human Node receives the original contributed capital less the applicable Risk Fee.

### 4.2 System 2: The Refill System (Continuous Sustainability)

Once the initial capital cycle is complete, the protocol transitions into the recurring Refill System, which generates continuous operational revenue from water refill activities.

**Distribution of Refill Operational Surplus:**

- **Project Management / LA2 (55%):** Supports day-to-day operations, manufacturing, maintenance of refill infrastructure, logistics, staffing, and continuous expansion of water access.
- **System Treasury / MVI1 (25%):** Funds ecosystem governance, protocol upgrades, regulatory compliance, and the launch of future MVIs.
- **Human Node (10%):** Continuous Impact Operational Grant. Provides ongoing operational compensation for maintaining liquidity access and supporting the protocol's long-term operational capacity.
- **MVI DApp Team Grant (10%):** Provides dedicated funding for continuous software maintenance, smart contract upgrades, security monitoring, technical innovation, and infrastructure reliability.

> **100% of the operational surplus** generated by the Refill System is allocated according to the protocol's predefined financial model.
